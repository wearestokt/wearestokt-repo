# Copywriting Expert — Reference

## Frameworks

### PAS (Problem → Agitate → Solution)
For short-form: ads, social, email subject lines, CTAs.

1. **Problem**: Name the reader's pain or desire in their words.
2. **Agitate**: Deepen it—what happens if they ignore it? What are they missing?
3. **Solution**: Your offer as the clear answer. One benefit, one CTA.

### AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action)
For sales pages, landing pages, long-form.

1. **Attention**: Headline that stops the scroll. Promise, curiosity, or bold claim.
2. **Interest**: Expand. Why does this matter? What's at stake?
3. **Desire**: Benefits, proof, social proof. Make them want it.
4. **Action**: Single, specific CTA. Remove friction.

### BAB (Before → After → Bridge)
For transformation stories, social, testimonials.

1. **Before**: Their current state (specific, relatable).
2. **After**: Their desired state (concrete outcome).
3. **Bridge**: Your product/service as the path from Before to After.

### FAB (Features → Advantages → Benefits)
For product descriptions, feature lists.

- **Feature**: What it is (spec, capability).
- **Advantage**: What it does (functional outcome).
- **Benefit**: What it means for the reader (emotional/practical payoff).

Always end on benefit. "Saves 10 hours/week" (benefit) beats "Automated workflow" (feature) alone.

### SCQA (Situation → Complication → Question → Answer)
For B2B, thought leadership, case studies.

1. **Situation**: Shared context. "Most teams struggle with X."
2. **Complication**: Why it's hard. "The usual approaches fail because..."
3. **Question**: The key question. "How do you X without Y?"
4. **Answer**: Your solution. "We built Z to..."

### StoryBrand
For brand positioning, messaging hierarchy.

- **Character**: The customer (hero), not the brand.
- **Problem**: External, internal, philosophical.
- **Guide**: The brand (empathy + authority).
- **Plan**: Simple steps. "Do these 3 things."
- **Call to Action**: Primary (buy/sign up) + Transitional (smaller commitment).
- **Success**: What life looks like after.
- **Failure**: What happens if they don't act.

## Banned Phrases (AI-isms)

Replace these with concrete, specific language:

| Avoid | Prefer |
|-------|--------|
| leverage | use, apply, tap into |
| transform | change, improve, turn X into Y |
| comprehensive | complete, full, covers X, Y, Z |
| robust | strong, reliable, built for X |
| seamless | smooth, easy, no-fuss |
| cutting-edge | new, latest, [specific tech/approach] |
| innovative | [describe what's new] |
| game-changing | [specific outcome it enables] |
| revolutionize | change how you X |
| empower | help, enable, let you |
| synergy | work together, combine |
| holistic | full, end-to-end, across X |
| scalable | grows with you, handles X users |
| best-in-class | [cite award, metric, or comparison] |
| world-class | [cite proof] |
| In today's fast-paced world | [Start with the reader's situation] |
| At the end of the day | [Cut or replace with the actual point] |
| It's important to note | [State the note directly] |

## Expert Principles

**Eugene Schwartz (Breakthrough Advertising)**
- Meet the reader at their level of awareness. Don't over-explain if they already know the problem.
- Use "mass desire" language—words that already resonate with the audience.
- Lead with the transformation, not the mechanism.

**David Ogilvy**
- Headlines do 80% of the work. Spend most time there.
- Specificity beats vagueness. "Increase sales by 37%" beats "Increase sales."
- Write like you talk. Read it aloud.

**Claude Hopkins**
- Offer a clear reason to act now (trial, guarantee, limited offer).
- Test one change at a time.
- Service in the ad—give useful information, not just hype.

**Gary Halbert**
- Write to one person. "You" not "people."
- Curiosity gaps work when the payoff is worth it.
- Swipe and adapt from what works in your niche.

## Document Extraction Checklist

When the user provides source documents, extract:

- [ ] **Brand voice**: 3–5 adjectives (e.g., warm, direct, playful, authoritative)
- [ ] **Audience**: Who, role, pain points, goals
- [ ] **Differentiators**: What makes this offer unique (from their words)
- [ ] **Proof**: Stats, testimonials, case studies, credentials
- [ ] **Forbidden words**: Terms to avoid (competitor language, overused, off-brand)
- [ ] **Example phrases**: Sentences or passages to emulate
- [ ] **Tone markers**: Formal/informal, jargon level, sentence length
